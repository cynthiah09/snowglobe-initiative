Who We Are

About SnowGlobe

A youth-led environmental initiative rooted in community, creativity, and care for our planet.

Our Mission

Why We Exist

The SnowGlobe Initiative empowers youth to take practical, community-based action against environmental waste through education, accessible resources, and local sustainability projects.

The Name

Where “SnowGlobe” Comes From

The name SnowGlobe was inspired by the perfect winter scene captured inside a snow globe and the noticeable lack of snowfall Vancouver has experienced over the past few winters. Seeing something so familiar begin to change made environmental issues feel closer to home.

The contrast between the snowy world preserved inside a snow globe and the winters outside reminded us that climate change is not only a distant problem. It is already affecting the communities we know. SnowGlobe grew from our desire to turn that awareness into practical, youth-led action through projects that make sustainability more approachable and accessible.

The Team

Meet the Founders

Cynthia Huang

Hi! I’m Cynthia, a rising senior in the fall, and as a co-founder of the SnowGlobe Initiative, I’ve always had a passion and love for the environment. One of my favourite hobbies is snowboarding, so part of the reason for starting the SnowGlobe Initiative was to try to take action against climate change as it is slowly taking over my sport. I hope that by taking action, we can make individuals in the community feel more empowered to take action and remind them that they can genuinely make a difference, no matter how big or small.

Bella Lu

Hi, I’m Bella! I’m a grade 12 student with a strong interest in sustainability, health, and community impact. I co-founded SnowGlobe because I wanted to turn environmental concerns into practical, welcoming projects that people can genuinely participate in. Through this initiative, I hope to make sustainable choices feel more accessible and encourage other young people to recognize that meaningful change can begin within their own communities.

General Volunteering

Help us out in working towards a more sustainable future!

What You'll Do

Volunteer Opportunities

As a SnowGlobe volunteer, you can get involved in the parts of our work that interest you most:

  • Helping run community workshops
  • Collecting plastics or textiles for our projects
  • Setting up events
  • Creating social media content
  • Supporting new sustainability initiatives

Volunteering is flexible, and there are different ways to contribute both behind the scenes and in the community.

Who Can Volunteer

Is This for Me?

SnowGlobe welcomes students in Grades 7–12 who are interested in sustainability, community involvement, or simply trying something new.

There are no mandatory weekly or monthly time commitments, so you can participate when opportunities fit your schedule, and no previous experience is needed. We’ll provide the information and support you need to get involved!

Start a School Chapter

Become a chapter leader to bringing SnowGlobe to your school!

What Is a Chapter

What It Means to Start a Chapter

Starting a SnowGlobe chapter means bringing our mission into your own school community.

Chapters are student-led groups that organize sustainability projects based on what matters most at their school. This could include:

  • Waste-reduction campaigns
  • Collection drives
  • Workshops
  • Fundraisers
  • School events
  • Other projects developed by your team

As a chapter leader, you’ll build a small team, communicate with your school, organize activities throughout the year, and stay connected with the main SnowGlobe team.

Chapters have the freedom to adapt projects to their own school while still being part of a larger network working toward the same goal.

The Process

How to Get Started

  1. Apply to start a chapter. Fill out our short chapter application and tell us a little about yourself, your school, and why you want to get involved.
  2. Connect with SnowGlobe. We’ll reach out to meet you, answer questions, and help you figure out what starting a chapter will look like at your school.
  3. Get approved by your school. Follow your school’s club process, find a teacher sponsor if required, and begin building your team.
  4. Plan your first project. We’ll help you choose or develop an activity that makes sense for your school and give you the resources to organize it.
  5. Launch your chapter. Recruit members, hold your first meeting or event, and officially introduce SnowGlobe to your school community.
  6. Stay connected. Share your progress with us, collaborate with other chapters, and continue planning projects throughout the year.
Support

What We Provide

SnowGlobe will provide chapter leaders with:

  • Starter resources
  • Project and workshop ideas
  • Promotional templates
  • Guidance for planning events
  • Ongoing support from our team

We will also help provide support in:

  • Troubleshooting challenges
  • Developing new initiatives
  • Connecting with other student leaders in the SnowGlobe network

You’ll have the freedom to make your chapter your own, with our team to provide support whenever necessary!

Partner With Us

Join us as a community partner and help amplify youth-led environmental action.

Why Partner

What Partnership Looks Like

Partnering with SnowGlobe can take many forms depending on what works for your organization. You might host a collection point for reusable materials, co-run a workshop or community event, sponsor supplies for a project, support one of our initiatives, or help us share sustainability efforts with a wider audience. We want partnerships to be collaborative and flexible, creating something that is meaningful for both your organization and the community.

Who We Partner With

Are You A Good Fit?

We’re excited to work with businesses, schools, cafés and food establishments, community organizations, and other groups that care about sustainability and local impact. You don’t need to have sustainability completely figured out. We especially value partners who are open to trying practical ways to reduce waste, support environmental education, or create more sustainable options within their communities.

Next Workshop

Eco-Squishy Workshop

  • DateSaturday, September 26
  • Time1:00–3:30 pm
  • LocationLily Lee Spirit Room, West Vancouver Community Centre

What You’ll Make

Make your own version of the viral crunchy squishies, using more eco-friendly materials. We’ll be working with biodegradable latex and plastic straws collected from local businesses that would otherwise have thrown them out, since BC’s single-use plastic rules mean they can no longer be handed to customers.

What You’ll Learn

Alongside making your squishy, we’ll talk about what “biodegradable” actually means, why some materials break down and others don’t, and how small changes in what we use and reuse add up to a real environmental difference.

Spotlight of the Week

Earnest Ice Cream

What They Do

Earnest Ice Cream builds social and environmental responsibility into how it operates. They use refillable glass jars and compostable, reusable, or edible packaging, source local and ethical ingredients when possible, maintain carbon-neutral operations, and power their production kitchen with renewable natural gas. They also donate 1% of their revenue to community causes, support local producers, advocate for climate action, and continue working toward becoming a zero-waste and more inclusive company.

Why They Stood Out

Earnest stood out to us because their sustainability efforts are both ambitious and easy for customers to take part in. Their jar return program has brought back more than 254,000 jars in a single year, showing how a simple reusable system can create a measurable impact. We also appreciated that they are open about continuing to learn and improve rather than treating sustainability as something they have already perfected.
